Refill requests are some of the most common calls a clinic gets, and some of the most frustrating. The patient leaves a message, a nurse calls back, nobody answers, and the cycle starts again.

## Collect everything on the first call

Halden never approves or changes a prescription. Its job is to make sure the nurse has everything needed to act without calling the patient back.

- Patient name, date of birth and callback number
- Medication name, dose and the pharmacy to send it to
- When the patient last took it and how many doses are left

## Know when to stop

Some refill calls aren’t really refill calls. If a patient mentions new symptoms, side effects or a controlled medication, Halden hands the call to a person straight away.

<Quote cite="Nurse manager, internal medicine clinic">“The request lands in our queue with everything filled in. We approve it and move on.”</Quote>

## Close the loop with the patient

Once the nurse acts, Halden texts the patient that the refill was sent – or asks them to book a visit if the doctor needs to see them first.

## What to copy

Agree with your clinicians which medications can never go through the refill flow, and write that list before you go live. Review it every quarter.
