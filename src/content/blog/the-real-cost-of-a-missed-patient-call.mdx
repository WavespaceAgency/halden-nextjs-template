Most clinics know they miss calls. Very few know how many, or what those calls are worth. Without a number, the phone problem always loses to the next urgent thing on the list.

## Count the calls you never see

Start with your phone system’s call log, not your gut. Pull four normal weeks and count three things: calls that rang out, calls that went to voicemail, and callers who hung up while on hold.

- Rang out or abandoned in the queue
- Went to voicemail and never got a call back the same day
- Called twice or more within an hour about the same thing

In most practices we work with, 20–30% of inbound calls fall into one of these buckets. Mondays and lunch hours are always the worst.

## Put a price on one call

Not every missed call is a lost visit. Some callers try again; some only wanted directions. A fair rule of thumb is that one in three missed calls from a new patient is a booking that goes to another clinic.

<Quote cite="Practice manager, family medicine group">“Once we multiplied it out, the phones were our biggest leak – bigger than no-shows.”</Quote>

Multiply that by the value of a first visit and the follow-ups it usually leads to. For a specialist clinic, a single lost new patient is often worth more than a month of the software that would have answered the call.

## Three fixes that move the number

You don’t need to fix everything at once. These three changes recover most of the lost calls in the first month.

- Answer every call in one ring, including lunch and after hours
- Book routine visits on the first call instead of taking a message
- Send a text with a booking link when a caller hangs up

## Measure it again in 30 days

Run the same count on the next four weeks. Share it with the whole team, not only the managers – the front desk usually has the best ideas for what to fix next.
