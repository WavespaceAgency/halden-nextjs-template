Sunrise Pediatrics has two people at the front desk and about 1,200 calls in a normal week. We spent five days next to them to see which calls Halden should take and which it should never touch.

## Monday: the sick-visit rush

By 8:15 the line was full of parents asking for same-day sick visits. Most calls followed the same pattern, which made them a good fit for the agent – with one firm rule.

- Book routine sick visits into the reserved same-day slots
- Send any call about breathing, fever in a newborn or injury straight to a nurse
- Never give medical advice, even when a parent asks for it

## Midweek: forms, shots and school notes

Tuesday to Thursday were quieter but full of small requests: vaccine records, sports physicals and school notes. Halden answered the routine questions and sent the paperwork requests to a shared inbox with a summary.

<Quote cite="Front-desk coordinator, Sunrise Pediatrics">“I finally had time to help the families standing in front of me.”</Quote>

## What we changed after

Parents often called for two or three children at once, so we added sibling booking to the script. And we moved the nurse hand-off earlier in the call – if a parent sounded worried, a person took over.
