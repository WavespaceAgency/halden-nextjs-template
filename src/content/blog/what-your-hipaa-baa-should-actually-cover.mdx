A Business Associate Agreement is the contract that makes a vendor responsible for protecting your patients’ health information. Most BAAs are copied from a template, and most of them say very little about how an AI vendor actually handles data.

## Start with what is in scope

The BAA should name every kind of PHI the vendor touches. For a phone agent, that is more than you might think.

- Call audio, recordings and transcripts
- Booking details written into your EHR
- Text messages and web chats with patients
- Summaries and notes created for your staff

## Ask how models use your data

This is the question most templates skip. Get it in writing that your data is not used to train models for other customers, and find out whether any outside AI provider sees it.

<Quote cite="Privacy officer, multi-site orthopaedic group">“The training clause was the first thing we checked. If a vendor can’t answer it clearly, we stop there.”</Quote>

## Sub-processors and where data lives

Every company that handles your data on the vendor’s behalf must be bound by the same terms. Ask for the list, and ask to be told before it changes.

- A current list of sub-processors with what each one does
- Notice before a new sub-processor is added
- Data stored in the US, encrypted in transit and at rest

## Breaches and the end of the contract

Check how fast the vendor must tell you about a breach – shorter than the legal maximum is better – and what happens to your data when you leave. You should be able to export it, and get written confirmation once it is deleted.

## A short checklist

Before you sign, make sure you can answer yes to each of these: PHI types are listed, training use is ruled out, sub-processors are named, breach notice is in days not weeks, and deletion is confirmed in writing.
